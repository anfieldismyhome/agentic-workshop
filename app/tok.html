Introduction

Hook & Context: Open with a real-life situation or observation that connects to your title. Then state your thesis — your overall answer to the prescribed title — and briefly outline how you will argue it across your chosen Areas of Knowledge.

Pointers
  • State your thesis clearly — what is your direct answer to the prescribed title?
  • Mention both Areas of Knowledge you will discuss
  • Define any key terms from the prescribed title
  • Aim for 150-200 words — concise and focused
  • Avoid vague statements like "knowledge is important"

Body Paragraph 1 — First Area of Knowledge

Claim & Evidence: Make a clear claim that supports your thesis using your first AOK. Support it with a specific real-life example, case study, or scenario. Explain how this example demonstrates your point and what it reveals about the knowledge question.
0 words
Pointers
  • Start with a clear topic sentence that makes a claim about the prescribed title
  • Use a specific, detailed real-life example — name names, dates, events
  • Analyse the example: what does it show about how knowledge works in this AOK?
  • Connect every sentence back to the prescribed title
  • Use TOK vocabulary: justification, evidence, methodology, perspective

Body Paragraph 2 — Second Area of Knowledge

Claim & Evidence: Now develop your argument through your second AOK. Make a claim that builds on or contrasts with your first paragraph. Use a different real-life example and analyse it thoroughly. How does this AOK offer a different perspective on the knowledge question?
0 words
Pointers
  • Ensure this paragraph adds something new — not just a repeat of paragraph 1
  • Consider how this AOK's methods, scope, or assumptions differ
  • Your example should be as specific and well-developed as the first
  • Show tension or contrast between the two AOKs for stronger analysis
  • Keep asking: "So what?" — what are the implications for the prescribed title?

Body Paragraph 3 — Counterclaim & Synthesis

Counterclaim & Synthesis: Present a counterclaim or alternative perspective that challenges your thesis. Then synthesise — how do the different AOK perspectives come together? What is the most nuanced answer to the prescribed title? This paragraph shows you can see multiple sides of the issue.
0 words
Pointers
  • A strong TOK essay acknowledges and addresses a counterclaim
  • The counterclaim should be genuine, not a straw man
  • Synthesise: don't just list both sides, show how they interact
  • This is where you demonstrate evaluative thinking — weighing competing claims
  • Use phrases like "However, one might argue that...", "On balance..."

Conclusion

Wrap Up & Answer: Summarise your argument without introducing new examples. Return directly to the prescribed title and give your final, considered answer. Reflect on the implications — what does your analysis say about the nature of knowledge itself?
0 words
Pointers
  • No new examples or evidence in the conclusion
  • Explicitly answer the prescribed title — this is your final position
  • Synthesise what the comparison of AOKs has revealed about knowledge
  • Consider a wider implication or a question your essay opens up
  • Aim for 150-200 words
